What is hard to retrofit

Five things that decide whether a space works.

Everything else in the program is ordinary medical-office fit-out. These are the items worth checking before anyone schedules a tour.

Screening criteria

  • Wet block, 800 SF. Two saunas, two cold plunges and steam, with the locker, shower and changing rooms sharing a wall with them. Needs floor drains, a reachable sanitary stack, waterproof membrane and dedicated exhaust. This is the most expensive thing to add to a building that was not built for it, and the first question we will ask about any space. Must have
  • A lobby that splits cleanly. The clinic is open to the public; the club is members only. A patient arriving for a treatment must not route through a locker room. In the plan below arrival is the hinge: the clinical corridor runs east off it to the treatment rooms and IV lounge, and the club runs north straight into the lounge and on to the wet block. The two never cross. Must have
  • Ground floor strongly preferred. Plunges, a hyperbaric chamber and a bone densitometer are heavy, and the wet areas make slab-on-grade drainage far simpler. Upper floors are possible but change the drainage and floor-loading conversation. Preferred
  • Clear ceiling height. Driven by the hyperbaric chamber and the sauna assemblies, plus exhaust runs above the wet block. Spec to follow
  • Electrical service. Saunas, hyperbaric, red light beds, the iDXA densitometer and clinical equipment run simultaneously, so we need a dedicated panel and several dedicated circuits. Load letter to follow

What we bring

  • Licensed, accredited operator. Medical director, injector and clinical leadership seated pre-opening. This is a licensed medical facility rather than a spa, and both the covenant and the fit-out standard reflect that.
  • Capitalised fit-out. Buildout and tenant improvements are budgeted at $250 per SF across the 4,925 SF program, with wet areas carried as a separate $465,000 line. We are not asking the landlord to underwrite the plunges.
  • Pre-committed revenue. 150 founding memberships sold before opening and collected during construction, so occupancy is not contingent on ramp.
  • Daytime, low-turnover traffic. Members on a booked circuit and scheduled clinical appointments. No walk-in retail volume, no late-night operation, and no food service beyond a staff breakroom.
  • Long term, high-specificity tenant. A facility built to this spec does not move.

Where the 4,925 square feet go.

Net program of 4,035 SF plus an 890 SF circulation and grossing factor. Areas are programmed areas measured to wall centreline, which is the convention a space program uses; interior clear area is marginally less once partitions are built. The illustrative envelope drawn above is 4,928 SF, and the 3 SF difference is rounding carried in circulation. In a real envelope the grossing factor is the number that moves: an awkward shape spends more of the total on corridor and less on rooms, which is why we screen for compact floor plates.

Adjacency requirements

  • Both contrast rooms share a wall with the locker room, but are entered from the corridor. Each holds one sauna and one cold plunge, and each opens onto member circulation, so nobody crosses a changing area to reach a sauna. The three rooms still sit as one 800 SF block on a single plumbing wall, because separating them doubles the wet rough-in.
  • The lounge opens directly off arrival. Members meet the club the moment they walk in, rather than being routed past it. The wet block sits behind the lounge off member circulation, and the circuit ends back in the lounge.
  • Arrival reaches the clinic without crossing the club. The clinical corridor runs directly east off arrival and serves all three treatment rooms.
  • Treatment rooms, consult rooms and the IV lounge form one suite. One clinical staff pool covers all three, and all six open directly onto the clinical corridor. Nobody crosses an occupied room to reach another, so a consult patient never walks through the infusion lounge.
  • Biohazard and linen on the service side. Drawn as an alcove off the MEP room, on back-of-house circulation and away from members.
  • MEP adjacent to the wet block riser. Shortest possible exhaust and hot-water runs.
